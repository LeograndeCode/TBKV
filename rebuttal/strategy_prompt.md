# Strategy prompt (apply to every reviewer before drafting)

This is the author's instruction, recorded for reuse. For each reviewer, produce a research-backed strategy before drafting any rebuttal text.

1. **Research.** Research rebuttal practice, using official AAAI policy for the current year first, then NeurIPS/ICML/ICLR/CVPR guidance and experienced researchers' advice. Cite sources, and label official policy separately from advice and anecdote.
2. **Analyse.** Analyse the reviewer's actual weaknesses and their "Specific Points of Feedback for Rebuttal".
3. **Classify each criticism.** Categories: factual/presentation error, missing method detail, reproducibility, missing evidence, fundamental weakness, unfavourable results, weak motivation, reviewer misunderstanding. For each, state:
   - the real concern;
   - how it can be resolved (clarify / correct / experiment / limitation / substantive change);
   - what evidence would convince;
   - its likely impact on the score;
   - how to address it without unsupported claims.
4. **Strategy.** Cover:
   - A. overall approach;
   - B. prioritisation (importance, urgency, ease, potential to change the assessment);
   - C. evidence to prepare (essential / recommended / optional);
   - D. the central performance criticism, evaluating six strategies (FLOPs, deployment scenario, extra measurements, narrowing claims, acknowledging, revising motivation), without inventing deployment advantages;
   - E. an experiment plan under time limits;
   - F. writing style, with labelled illustrative examples.
5. **Action plan.** List first actions, evidence, manuscript changes, order, rebuttal structure and a checklist. Separate four kinds of item:
   - immediate corrections;
   - implementation checks;
   - experiments;
   - fundamental concerns.

Rules:
- Don't fabricate statistics or acceptance probabilities.
- Don't assume that addressing every comment guarantees acceptance.
- Don't draft the rebuttal until the strategy is set.
- Be candid about weaknesses.
